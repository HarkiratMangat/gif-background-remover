## 3. The hurricane investigation, part one: was `--recover-fade-alpha` even the right call?

1. First concluded "current opaque treatment is correct" by quoting the tool's own historical `lessons.md §34.2` measurement — `bg_removed_worst` (a score measuring how completely true-background pixels get reduced to alpha 0 across all frames; `0.39` means a large fraction of background stayed visibly semi-opaque, i.e. a bad result) read `0.39` when `--recover-fade-alpha` was forced on this class of asset in a past investigation. Quoted without checking fresh data on THIS specific asset. **Harkirat: "ignore the history, work using your present knowledge."**
2. Redid the analysis "fresh" with a **flawed single-fixed-pixel probe** that happened to land on the outline colour at some sampled frames, producing a spuriously clean "converges exactly to pure white" result that supported the wrong conclusion. Corrected by switching to a robust per-frame dominant-colour trace (75 distinct values across the animation, smooth, genuinely real motion, no discrete jumps — this WAS a real translucency effect).

4. The outline's OWN paler stages (`2f377d, 5a4f8c, 79679c, 937d99, 9f8bb3, bdabc8` — cosine 0.978–0.998 with `052a75`) were themselves stuck as frozen "solid" colours: the exact same bug one level deeper, on the outline's own gradient this time instead of the two fill halves'.
5. Traced the mechanism precisely by reading `build_art_palette`'s actual rejection math (`FADE_RESIDUAL_TOLERANCE = 10.0`, tested per-candidate against already-accepted entries) — confirmed a real hand-animated fade's stages routinely sit more than 10 units off the exact background-to-colour blend line and survive as independent palette entries even when clearly the same physical fading design element.

## 4. Part two — Defect A: a wall made of the fading family's own colours can seal off its own interior

**Delivered the part-one fix to Harkirat, captioned "v2."** Defect A itself was found by me, not by Harkirat — a programmatic scan of the delivered render, done before showing him anything further. (The verbatim "outline flashing white" quote used in §6 belongs to Defect B, found later, after Defect A's fix had already shipped — keep the two chronologically separate: Defect A was self-discovered via a spike-detector scan; Defect B was reported by Harkirat after looking at the Defect-A-fixed render.)

**How Defect A was actually found:** a programmatic scan across all 120 frames for "fraction of art pixels rendering at exactly alpha=255" found sharp spikes at frames 53, 58, 74, 78 (values jumping from ~0.2–0.5 to 0.88–0.98 and back down within one frame) — a signature inconsistent with a smooth fade. Zoomed into frame 53: a 22,801px chunk of source colour `fd9287` (a genuinely pale, correctly-fading shade near `fd6050`) was rendering at `alpha=255` (fully opaque) despite `unmix_against_palette` computing a perfectly good `t=0.686, res=0.63` for it (well inside the 10.0 residual tolerance) — meaning the per-pixel colour math was RIGHT, but something else was overriding it to full opacity.

**Root cause, found by direct pixel/topology tracing, not by pattern-matching to a prior theory:** `recover_fade_alpha_frames`'s protection mechanism is **topological** — flood-fill reachability from the canvas border, through a "barrier" mask, determines which pixels get their computed alpha applied (`bgside`) versus defaulting to `alpha=255` (protected). This is deliberately how the tool protects real interior design (crystal.gif's white interior is colorimetrically *identical* to true background and can only be told apart by staying enclosed). But the barrier had no notion that an enclosing wall pixel can itself be part of the SAME fading family as the pixels it encloses. Two distinct mechanisms produce this:

- **(a) Art-to-art seams misread as "unexplained."** `unmix_against_palette` only ever tests ONE palette colour blended with the BACKGROUND — it has no hypothesis for "this pixel is a blend of two ART colours" (e.g. the navy outline touching the pink fill). Such a seam pixel gets high residual (`res > FADE_RESIDUAL_TOLERANCE`), which the code classifies as an impermeable "unexplained" barrier. During hurricane's fast shrink/regrow tumble frames, this seam ring gets thick enough (motion blur / heavier antialiasing during rapid scale change) to seal off a chunk of the badge's own fading interior on the far side. **Fix:** new function `fading_seam_mask()` at `scripts/remove_gif_background.py:5325` — for a pixel with high single-colour residual, additionally test it against every PAIR of named fading colours (a genuine two-art-colour blend line); if a pair explains it within tolerance, the pixel is a "seam" and is excluded from the barrier. **This is a no-op whenever fewer than two colours are named** (`if len(fading_idx) < 2: return zeros`) — which covers every one of the four corpus assets in §3, since they each name only one fading colour. Wired into both the `art_prior` accumulation loop and the main per-frame barrier computation.
- **(b) Pockets sealed only by non-solid barrier pixels.** Even after (a), a pocket can still be enclosed by a barrier made ENTIRELY of pixels that are neither genuinely solid art (`solid & t >= FADE_BARRIER_ALPHA`) nor the existing near-opaque-fading occlusion rule (`t >= FADE_OPAQUE_BLOCK & ~solid & art_prior >= FADE_ART_PRIOR`, the pre-existing "rule 3" built for crystal.gif's opaque-sparkle-over-navy case). For such a pocket, re-test its reachability using ONLY genuinely solid barriers (i.e. exclude the ambiguous/ring contribution entirely for this second pass). **If it becomes reachable under that looser test, AND less than 50% of the pocket is background-degenerate** (`t < FADE_SEAL_BG_T = 0.05`, `FADE_SEAL_BG_MAX = 0.5` — the two constants at `scripts/remove_gif_background.py:5287-5292`; crystal's genuine protected-white-interior pockets measure 97%+ background-degenerate, hurricane's genuine fading pockets measure under 8%) **AND the pocket is at least 50px** (`FADE_SEAL_MIN_SIZE = 50`, `scripts/remove_gif_background.py:5294` — excludes dozens of individually-trivial 1px AA specks found right on crystal.gif's sparkle edge that each pass the background-fraction test on their own and, if promoted, left a thin mis-coloured rim; this was caught by a real pixel diff on crystal, not assumed away), the pocket is exposed (`outside = outside | promote`). The actual promote loop is at `scripts/remove_gif_background.py:5837-5850` (`enclosed = (~barrier) & (~outside)` through `outside = outside | promote`).

**Two attempts before this shipped, both real, both caught by measurement, both reverted the same turn they were disproven — keep this reasoning in mind before proposing a similar "just check X's frequency" idea again:**

- **First attempt:** gate the plain "unexplained" barrier term on `art_prior` (a pixel-POSITION frequency signal — "how often is real art at this exact canvas position across the whole animation" — reusing the existing rule-3 signal). This broke crystal.gif for real: 13,913px wrongly exposed in a single frame (frame 42), a large contiguous chunk of crystal's genuinely protected white interior. **Why:** crystal's own outline shifts position slightly frame to frame too (it's not perfectly static), so no single canvas position has consistently-high `art_prior` for "the ring lives here" — the exact same problem as hurricane's spinning icon. A position-frequency prior fundamentally cannot distinguish "nothing protected ever lives here" from "protected content lives here, just not always at the same pixel." Measured, understood, reverted within the same turn.
- **Second (correct) attempt** is what's described above — a per-frame, per-CONNECTED-COMPONENT check using the component's own colour composition (background-degenerate fraction) rather than a cross-frame position average. This does not suffer from the motion problem because it's evaluated fresh on each frame's own actual pixel content.

**Corpus-validated identically to §3, at every stage including the false start:** crystal/gift/love/heart rendered with `--fade-color` on pre-fix and post-fix script, alpha planes diffed exactly, every time a code change was made. Final state: **0 of 461 combined frames differ.**

**One more false start inside Defect A's own fix, caught before it was ever shown to Harkirat:** the first working version of the seal-promotion check (the `(t < FADE_SEAL_BG_T).mean() >= FADE_SEAL_BG_MAX` test) had no minimum-size floor. Rendering crystal.gif with it found a small but real regression — 322px changed across 3 frames, all clustered in dozens of individually-tiny (mostly 1px) connected components sitting right on the sparkle's own antialiased edge. Each tiny component passed the background-degenerate-fraction test trivially on its own (a single non-background-colour pixel is, tautologically, under 50% background-degenerate) and got promoted, leaving a thin mis-coloured rim visible on close zoom. Adding `FADE_SEAL_MIN_SIZE = 50` (§4's constant list above) closed this — re-rendered crystal, 0px difference. This is the "size-floor addition" row in §9's ledger.

## 6. Part four — Defect B: the family-absorption threshold was too strict for a bevel highlight

**Delivered the Defect A fix, captioned "v3."** Harkirat, after looking closely at frames 30-40 of a sprite sheet: *"the actual red/pink fade out and fade in seems perfect. 1 thing im still noticing not fixed/buggy is the outline. same 30-40 frames in a sprite, notice how some frames show the navy outline flashing white or part of it flashing/turning white while the other part if it continues to fade?"*

**Investigation:** an automated scan using the strict "alpha=255 AND near-white RGB" criterion found nothing in the delivered v3/v4 render — meaning the defect was more subtle than a hard flash-to-opaque-white. Zoomed visually into frame 35's outer octagon border ring (composited against a dark, then a mid-tone backdrop) and saw a genuine asymmetry: one side of the border rendered as a clearly paler/whiter band than the other, a visible discontinuity rather than a smooth gradient.

**Root cause, found by sampling exact RGB/alpha values at 6 then 24 points around the ring:** two DISTINCT, very close but not identical, exact source colours make up this ring:
- `#cfc1d7`/`#207,193,215` — correctly absorbed (cosine 0.9799 with the named `042a75`), rendering at `alpha=115`, a genuinely partial fade.
- `#d4c4ce`/`212,196,206` — NOT absorbed (cosine **0.9607** with `042a75`, cosine 0.9366 with `da4b85` — both below the 0.97 floor used in §3's fix), rendering at `alpha=255`, fully opaque, on the opposite side of the border from the correctly-fading colour.

This is the outer octagon BORDER's own bevel-highlight shade — a separate design element from the inner pinwheel's outline, but drawn in the same `042a75` colour family — sitting just under the absorption threshold by a narrow margin (0.9607 vs 0.97).

**Fix:** widened the absorption cosine floor from 0.97 to **0.95** at `scripts/remove_gif_background.py:5681` (`if cos >= 0.95:`), with the reasoning comment updated at lines ~5644-5661. This is still comfortably above the 0.762–0.93 ceiling measured for every genuinely-distinct, non-fading real colour checked by hand on this specific asset (per the original `e9f8520` commit's own measurement), so the change doesn't loosen the discriminator past what real design colours on this asset ever reach.

**Important scope note:** this absorption loop, unlike `fading_seam_mask()`, is **NOT gated by how many colours are named** — it runs even for a single named `--fade-color`, since it only needs one named "parent" to search for paler collinear candidates around. This means the 0.95 threshold change genuinely needed a fresh corpus check, not an assumption that "it was safe before, it's still safe now."

**Corpus-validated again, at the new threshold:** crystal/gift/love/heart re-rendered with `--fade-color` at the 0.95 floor, diffed against the pre-session baseline exactly. **Still 0/461 frames differ.**

**Result on hurricane:** re-sampled the same 24 points around the border ring on frame 35 after the fix. `d4c4ce` now renders at `alpha=212` (partial, no longer stuck opaque) instead of 255. Frame 78's residual (§5) dropped further. A full labelled sprite sheet of frames 25-49, composited against both a mid-tone and a black backdrop, showed a smooth, consistent navy border across the entire shrink/regrow window with no visible hard flash-to-white anywhere. Delivered to Harkirat as a `.webp` file; the delivery-file-naming details are not load-bearing and are omitted here — what matters is that the code state described in this section (0.95 threshold, corpus-validated) is exactly what's committed in `5940a30`.

## 7. Part five — Defect C: an OPEN, deliberately-unfixed structural limitation (border banding)

Immediately after Defect B's fix was delivered, Harkirat sent a cropped screenshot pointing out the border STILL wasn't perfectly smooth — annotating it "90% fixed but not fully there yet."

**Investigation:** sampled 24 points around the border ring at 15° steps on frame 35 (the same method as Defect B). Found the alpha values step in discrete bands rather than ramping smoothly: `43 → 50 → 93–96 → 113–115 → 212`, not a continuous gradient. Traced this to `unmix_against_palette`'s fundamental matching strategy: **every pixel is matched to whichever SINGLE palette colour fits it best** (`k = r2.argmin(1)` inside `unmix_against_palette`, `scripts/remove_gif_background.py:5289-5309`). Several genuinely-distinct pale shades sit close together in this specific region of colour space (the border's own antialiasing against multiple adjacent fill colours at different fade stages); each one IS being unmixed correctly against its own true nearest match — but adjacent pixels can snap to DIFFERENT "nearest" winners, producing visible bands instead of one smooth ramp.

**Why this is categorically different from Defects A and B, and was not attempted tonight:** A and B were both miscategorizations — a pixel or a colour was being classified into the wrong bucket (barrier vs. not, fading vs. solid), and the fix was a bounded rule change (a permeability test, a threshold value) inside `recover_fade_alpha_frames`'s existing barrier logic. Defect C is not a miscategorization at all — every pixel's classification and unmix result are already correct given the tool's current strategy. Fixing it would require changing the strategy itself: e.g. a soft/weighted blend across the k-nearest fading-family palette entries instead of a hard single-winner match, or a proper multi-colour barycentric unmix. That is a change to `unmix_against_palette`'s core algorithm — used by every `--recover-fade-alpha` render, not just hurricane's — and would need its OWN corpus validation pass against crystal/gift/love/heart (and ideally the broader corpus) before being trusted, exactly like every other change tonight was.

**Given the hour (past midnight) and the size of that redesign, Harkirat explicitly chose to stop:** offered via `AskUserQuestion` — "keep going now" vs. "stop here for tonight (recommended)" — he chose to stop. This is documented here specifically so the next session starts the redesign with full context rather than rediscovering the banding, the root cause, or why it wasn't just threshold-patched again.

**Concretely, for whoever picks this up:** the entry point is `unmix_against_palette()` at line 5289. Any redesign must (a) preserve exact behaviour for `res <= FADE_RESIDUAL_TOLERANCE` single-colour matches with only one plausible winner — the vast majority of real pixels — and (b) only change behaviour in the ambiguous region where two-plus fading-family colours are all near-equally good fits. A first idea worth testing: for pixels where the top-2 candidate residuals are within some small margin of each other, blend their `(colour, alpha)` outputs by inverse-residual weight rather than taking the single argmin. This has NOT been prototyped or measured — it is a starting idea, not a validated approach.

## 8. Real mistakes made this session, each caught and corrected — read before repeating any of them

This mechanism worked exactly as designed and is why that job's results were trustworthy despite the live file being swapped out from under it mid-run. **This incident, not #7, is the one to actually worry about generalizing from**: #7 was a scratch/output folder; this one was the actual product source file the whole session's work lived in, and the failure mode (a slow background job holding a stale restore step) is a new pattern worth watching for specifically — any script that does "backup → mutate → long-running operation → restore" on a file you might edit again before the operation finishes is unsafe unless the restore is guaranteed to happen before the next edit, not merely "eventually."
