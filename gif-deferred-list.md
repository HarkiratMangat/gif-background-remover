### `[P2 · M · Opus5-High]` A soft glow on a dark background has no single fading colour, so the fade path cannot see it *(filed 2026-08-20, from the §35 fix)*

Surfaced by the fix above, not by it: with `--recover-fade-alpha` correctly suppressed, `local/corpus dark/_ (17).gif` — a neon streak with a soft outer glow on flat black — takes the normal removal path and loses the glow's outer falloff. `art_kept_worst` **1.000 → 0.761** (51,243 art pixels of a 69,671 peak, at frame 14), `art_lost_over_perimeter` **2.972**, `edge_cleanliness` 0.466 → 0.213. The previous output preserved the falloff only because it preserved *everything* at partial alpha, so this is a real trade rather than a pure regression — but the falloff is genuinely part of the artwork.

⚠️ **THIS IS ONE OF THREE OPEN ITEMS WITH THE SAME ROOT**, and they should be read together before any of them is worked: this one, the `--recover-fade-alpha` CLIFF item below (a solid PALE shape read as translucent), and AUTONOMY BACKLOG item 5 (one colour that is simultaneously solid artwork and a translucent element). All three are `detect_fading_colors` failing to describe a translucent element correctly, in three different directions. Fixing one in isolation risks re-breaking another.

**Why the detector cannot see it.** `detect_fading_colors` looks for a palette colour appearing as a large region at a flat PARTIAL alpha. A glow ramps through many colours, none of which is one flat entry, so it clears no threshold. This is the same boundary `references/lessons.md` §34.2 records from the other side — there, *pale* was wrongly read as *translucent*; here, genuinely translucent art is invisible because it is not monochrome.

**Do:** decide whether a multi-colour ramp is in scope for fade recovery at all. If it is, the detector needs a different shape (a colour *ramp* toward the background, not a single colour at partial alpha) — measure it against §34.2's hurricane case, which must NOT get worse. If it is not, say so in `--recommend`'s evidence so a run knows the falloff will be cut. ⚠️ `--fade-color` already bypasses detection and is the manual escape hatch; check whether naming one stage of the ramp is enough before building anything.

### `[P2 · S · Opus5-High]` Edge-cleanup erosion deletes thin features on a dark background — nearly 2× the perimeter *(filed 2026-08-20 from the dark corpus)*

`local/corpus dark/Team Valor Animation #PokemonGO.gif`, a flat black background: `art_kept_worst 0.2399`, `interior_kept_worst 0.0000`. **The worst frame holds 0.54% of the animation's peak artwork — a thin flame wisp — and `art_lost_over_perimeter` is 1.977.**

⚠️ **That ratio is what makes this a real finding rather than the collapsed-denominator artefact filed the same day.** A 1px erosion cannot cost more than about one perimeter ring; measured across the white-background trial assets it ran 0.70–0.97. Here it is nearly TWO rings, which is the signature of a feature so thin it is bitten from both sides at once. Same field, opposite verdict — which is the point of having built it.

⚠️ **MEASURED AND ATTEMPTED TWICE ON 2026-08-20; BOTH FIXES FAILED THEIR OWN ACCEPTANCE AND WERE REVERTED. Read `references/lessons.md` §37 before trying a third — it records both dead ends in full.** The reach is now known rather than guessed: 149 assets rendered through the real `--auto` CLI put **47 of 148 over `art_lost_over_perimeter` 1.1**, and re-rendering each of those with erosion off attributes **36 of 46 to erosion and 10 to the keyer**, which no erosion change would touch. The control is known too: of 41 assets already under 1.1, turning erosion off makes `edge_cleanliness` **worse on 9** (`for-you.gif` 1.000 → 0.688) and leaves background behind on 7, so a blunt reduction is refused by its own evidence.

**What was tried and why each failed.** (1) Reusing `check_erosion_damage` as a gate: its 25px/30%-survival bar was tuned for a WARNING and fires on the dither speckle erosion is supposed to remove — `edge_cleanliness` worse on **40 of 147**, better on 2. (2) A scale-free `lost / perimeter` overreach measure: it separates the two populations cleanly at 1.2 with zero false positives, and is **arithmetically incapable of firing** — for one iteration the pixels erosion removes ARE the boundary ring, so the ratio is exactly 1.000 on every input, and the clean split was really separating erosion-2/3 assets from erosion-1 assets.

**Do:** the cost term must compare erosion's loss against something that is not itself a function of erosion. Untried: loss against the frame's artwork AREA rather than its boundary; per-component survival with a floor derived from the asset's own component-size distribution rather than a constant 25px; or conceding it is a per-asset judgement and having `--recommend` say so. ⚠️ **The acceptance is fixed and both halves are required**: the 36 erosion-caused assets improve AND `edge_cleanliness` does not fall on the 41 controls. ⚠️ **AND ADD `alphas` TO IT FIRST.** The 149-asset measurement behind this item covers `labelled`, `trial`, `corpus` and `dark_bg` — it does NOT include the already-background-removed population, which is exactly the blind spot that let the changing-background rule ship refusing 9 of those 37 assets on 2026-08-20 (`references/lessons.md` §36). A third cost term validated on the same four populations would inherit the same hole. Re-check against the white assets, where erosion 1 is measurably correct on 2 of 5 (`references/lessons.md` §34.5).

### `[P1 · L (first slice: M) · Opus5-High]` A 40-frame spread cannot see a transient, and the trigger for looking harder cannot be computed from the spread *(filed 2026-08-20, Harkirat's question)*

`analyze(max_samples=40)` samples the expensive per-frame work — the candidate-region enclosure search, edge hardness, the band-interior/fade detection. Several checks already scan every frame (tumble margin, the small-region histogram, the blank-frame scan added 2026-08-20), so this is about the sampled half only.

**The failure is a RATIO, not a class of asset: a transient of length L in an N-frame file is invisible when `40/N < 1/L`.** Measured 2026-08-20 — `for-you.gif` (144 frames) loses its navy outline for **37 consecutive frames** and a 40-sample spread lands ~10 samples inside it, so it was caught; `in-love.gif` (48 frames) has fade stages living 1–2 frames each and 40-of-48 samples nearly everything; but the changing-background asset filed the same morning has a 5-frame window in 30, and a 10-sample scan read **78.6% at frame 10 while frame 12 was 0.0%**. The same content at 300 frames misses all three.

⚠️ **So a "this asset looks hard" trigger computed FROM the 40 samples is unfalsifiable in the direction that matters** — the assets needing escalation most are exactly the ones whose difficulty is invisible at 40 samples. Do not build that.

**Do: make the CHEAP pass dense and let it nominate frames for the EXPENSIVE pass.** The blank-frame scan is the worked example — every frame, no measurable cost, because what makes `analyze` slow is the enclosure search, not decoding. One extra cheap pass over every frame collecting the detected background colour, the pixel count of each provisional palette colour, and the non-background pixel count yields four nominations, **three of which are defects already filed here and currently undetected**:
1. **background colour MOVES** beyond tolerance → the changing-colour-background item above.
2. **a colour that carried the outline COLLAPSES** on a run of frames → `for-you.gif`; today it survives only because `--recommend` happened to pick a colour present in all 144 frames.
3. **a colour appears in very FEW frames at LARGE pixel counts** → `in-love.gif`'s fade stages; this is the fade-ladder signature from `references/lessons.md` §34.2.
4. **non-background count hits ZERO** → the blank frame. Already shipped.

Each yields FRAME INDICES, so escalation adds a handful of frames to the spread instead of raising 40 → N.

**First slice (M):** the cheap pass plus nomination (1) only — the changing-background item — with its cost measured on the longest asset in the corpus and the nomination shown to fire on `Pew Pew Pew.gif`. ⚠️ **A nomination that cannot flag its own motivating asset is not a signal**, and **the dense pass must be MEASURED cheap, not assumed cheap by analogy** with the blank-frame scan, which reused a mask that already existed. Changing `sample_idxs` feeds the hardness measures, so this forces a full 797-asset re-score.

### `[P1 · S · Opus5-High]` `--recover-fade-alpha` maps colour-distance to alpha as a CLIFF, not a ramp *(filed 2026-08-19)*

Measured on `hurricane.gif`, sampling the octagon fill between the shuriken and the edge:

| frame | source distance from white | output alpha |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 347.8 | 255 |
| 12 | 258.7 | **255** |
| 20 | 197.8 | 140.5 |
| 32 | 105.1 | 70.5 |
| 40 | 44.3 | 24.2 |

The source colour moves smoothly; alpha holds flat at 255 through frame 12 then drops to 140 within eight frames. Reviewed by eye as "super glitchy and buggy… despite it being a gradual smooth fadeout in the original". **All three trial agents produced BYTE-IDENTICAL output here** (same SHA over every frame), so this is one product behaviour, not a usage difference.

⚠️ **UPDATED 2026-08-20 — the mechanism was FOUND and the obvious fix was BUILT, MEASURED and REJECTED. Do not re-derive it.** The palette is a fade LADDER: `protect_parents` exempts every detected fading colour from `build_art_palette`'s blend rejection, and on this asset the detector flags **8 of 10** colours, so the exemption inverts and the fade's own stages survive as palette entries — one navy at distances **356.2, 316.5, 267.3, 223.5, 195.7, 155.6 and 105.8** from the background, each within cosine 0.97–0.999 of a farther rung, against 0.55 and 0.69 for the two unrelated art colours. Every faded pixel unmixes against its OWN rung at t≈1.0 and renders opaque. Collapsing the ladder dropped four rungs correctly and moved fade coherence **0.688 → 0.477**, with background (0.390) and edge (0.000) unchanged — worse on the axis it targeted, no gain anywhere else. The acceptance criterion was set at >0.90 *before* any tuning, so it was reverted. **A fix that removes a genuine defect can still leave the output further from correct than it started.** Full write-up: `references/lessons.md` §34.2. The ladder is real and is not the whole cause; whatever closes this needs a different mechanism.

⚠️ **The deeper error, and it may not be fixable by tuning:** `--recover-fade-alpha` treats *pale* as *translucent*. Hurricane's badge is a solid pale shape the animator drew getting lighter, not a see-through one. Reconstructing alpha from paleness composites identically over white and wrongly over anything else. **For a fade drawn against the background colour, alpha recovery and colour fidelity are different goals**, and the tool currently assumes they are the same. `references/lessons.md` §16 is the existing evidence trail.

The last sprite pack below 100%. The seventh discriminator (§32.7) took `Tiny RPG Character Asset Pack` from 0.235 to 0.941 and did not move this one. ### `[P2 · M · Opus5-High]` Re-analysing ANY GIF this tool writes classifies antialiased art as pixel art — 8.3% with no compression at all *(filed 2026-08-18; retitled twice the same day as the sample grew 3 → 14 → 48)*

**Added 2026-08-18 by the v5.5.0 audit pass, asking what a new measure does to the tool's OWN output.** `--compress heavy` quantizes colours, and quantization gives a 1px antialiasing ramp flat neighbours it did not have — so `plateau_cliff_ratio`'s founding premise ("a 1px ramp cannot be a cliff, because the ramp pixel is a plateau of length 1 by construction") is a property of the FILE, not of the artwork. Measured, source → its own `--compress heavy` output: `gem.gif` 305 colours/not-hard-edged → 122/**hard-edged** (cliff 0.324); `heart_ORIGINAL.gif` 320/not-hard-edged → 108/**hard-edged** (cliff 0.343); `add.png` unchanged. Two of three flip. Reachable in an ordinary workflow — compress for a size limit, then run the tool again on the output — and it is the likeliest explanation of the two false positives that survive in the corpus, both already-processed cutouts caught by the cliff ratio.

**The obvious veto was measured and rejected, so do not re-derive it.** Over the 451 assets the cliff rule detects correctly, colour count is median 12 / p95 20 but reaches 50, 56, 98, 123, 172, 221, 242 and 1,233 — dithered and photographic pixel art is exactly what that rule exists to reach. A veto at >100 (needed to catch the 108/122 cases) removes **0 of 2** corpus false positives and costs 5 real detections; at >80 it removes 1 of 2 and costs 6. Not implemented.

⚠️ **2026-08-18 — MEASURED ON 48 ASSETS, and both this item's original title and its first correction were wrong.** Every asset run through the real CLI at all four tiers and re-analysed: **`none` (no compression, the control) flips 4/48 = 8.3%**, `optimize` **12/48 = 25.0%**, `medium` 10/48 = 20.8%, `heavy` 6/48 = 12.5%. **14 of 48 (29.2%) flip at some tier, and `heavy` is the LEAST affected.** Nearly every flip is `plateau_cliff_ratio` clearing 0.30 (0.303–0.573 over 2,150–16,104 steps).
